Austria: 1960-2000

1960s - Neutrality and Prosperity

Austria maintained its neutral status established in 1955, balancing between East and West during the Cold War. The economy grew rapidly, transforming from agricultural to industrial. The "grand coalition" between the two major parties (SPÖ and ÖVP) governed through consensus. Vienna became a center for international diplomacy, hosting UN agencies. Tourism expanded significantly. The South Tyrol question with Italy was resolved (1969). Living standards rose dramatically as Austria rebuilt from wartime devastation.

1970s - Kreisky Era

Bruno Kreisky became chancellor (1970-1983), leading the Social Democrats to absolute majorities. His government expanded the welfare state, reformed education, and liberalized social policies. Austria became known for active neutrality and Middle East diplomacy. The economy remained strong despite oil shocks. Vienna hosted the OPEC headquarters and became a major international conference center. Women's rights advanced. The Kreisky era represented Austria's social democratic peak.

1980s - Waldheim Affair

Kurt Waldheim's election as president (1986) sparked international controversy over his Wehrmacht service and knowledge of war crimes. The affair forced Austria to confront its Nazi past more honestly. The grand coalition returned to power. The Green Party entered parliament (1986). Jörg Haider became leader of the far-right Freedom Party (1986), beginning its rise. Economic growth continued. The Iron Curtain's fall (1989) ended Austria's border position, opening opportunities eastward.

1990s - EU Membership

Austria joined the European Union (1995) after a referendum, ending its traditional neutrality interpretation. The economy integrated deeply with the EU. The Freedom Party's rise continued, entering coalition government (2000), causing EU sanctions. Immigration and identity became contentious issues. Vienna remained a major international center. The economy prospered, though unemployment rose modestly. The schilling was replaced by the euro (1999, circulation 2002). Austria positioned itself as a bridge to Central and Eastern Europe.
