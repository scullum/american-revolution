France: 1960-2000

1960s - De Gaulle and Decolonization

Charles de Gaulle led France through the Fifth Republic, pursuing an independent foreign policy. Algeria gained independence in 1962 after a bitter war. France developed its own nuclear weapons (1960) and withdrew from NATO's integrated military command (1966). Economic modernization accelerated. The May 1968 protests and general strikes nearly toppled the government, combining student activism with labor unrest. De Gaulle resigned in 1969 after losing a referendum.

1970s - Modernization and Change

Georges Pompidou (1969-1974) continued modernization and improved relations with Britain, supporting its EEC membership. Valéry Giscard d'Estaing became president (1974), pursuing liberal economic reforms. The voting age was lowered to 18 (1974). Divorce by mutual consent was legalized (1975). The oil crisis caused economic difficulties. France maintained its independent nuclear deterrent and continued European integration efforts.

1980s - Mitterrand Era

Socialist François Mitterrand won the presidency (1981), the first left-wing president of the Fifth Republic. Initial nationalizations and social reforms were followed by economic austerity. "Cohabitation" began in 1986 when the right won parliamentary elections, with Jacques Chirac as prime minister. The TGV high-speed rail network expanded. France celebrated its bicentennial (1989). The Maastricht Treaty (1992) was narrowly approved by referendum.

1990s - European Integration

Mitterrand continued as president until 1995. Jacques Chirac won the presidency (1995). France conducted controversial nuclear tests in the Pacific (1995-1996). The 35-hour work week was introduced (1998). France was a founding member of the Eurozone, adopting the Euro (1999). The country experienced economic growth but also rising unemployment and social tensions. France won the FIFA World Cup on home soil (1998), a moment of national unity.
