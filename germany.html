Germany: 1960-2000

1960s - Division and Prosperity

West Germany experienced the "Wirtschaftswunder" (economic miracle) under Chancellor Konrad Adenauer, then Ludwig Erhard. The Berlin Wall was constructed in 1961, physically dividing East and West Berlin. West Germany established diplomatic relations with Israel (1965). The Grand Coalition formed in 1966 between CDU/CSU and SPD. Student protests and the rise of the New Left marked the late 1960s. East Germany remained under communist rule as part of the Soviet bloc.

1970s - Ostpolitik and Terrorism

Chancellor Willy Brandt's Ostpolitik policy sought reconciliation with Eastern Europe, earning him the Nobel Peace Prize (1971). The Basic Treaty (1972) normalized relations between East and West Germany. The 1972 Munich Olympics were marred by a terrorist attack. The Red Army Faction (Baader-Meinhof Group) conducted terrorist attacks. Helmut Schmidt became chancellor (1974). Economic challenges emerged following the oil crisis.

1980s - Reunification

Helmut Kohl became chancellor in 1982, beginning a 16-year tenure. The Green Party entered the Bundestag (1983). East Germany faced economic stagnation and political unrest. Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms in the Soviet Union encouraged change. Mass demonstrations in East Germany (1989) led to the fall of the Berlin Wall on November 9, 1989. German reunification was achieved on October 3, 1990, merging the Federal Republic and the German Democratic Republic.

1990s - Unified Germany

The newly unified Germany faced challenges integrating the former East German states. Berlin was designated as the capital (1991), with government relocation completed by 1999. Germany played a key role in European integration, supporting the Maastricht Treaty (1992) and the creation of the European Union. The Deutsche Mark was replaced by the Euro in financial markets (1999). Economic restructuring in the east proved costly. Gerhard Schröder's SPD-Green coalition won elections in 1998, ending Kohl's era.
