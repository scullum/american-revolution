Greece: 1960-2000

1960s - Instability and Dictatorship

Political instability marked the early 1960s with conflicts between the monarchy and elected governments. The Colonels' Coup (April 21, 1967) established a military junta that suspended democracy. The regime was authoritarian and repressive, imprisoning and torturing opponents. King Constantine II fled after a failed counter-coup (1967). Economic growth continued despite political repression. Tourism began expanding. The junta's rule isolated Greece from democratic Europe.

1970s - Democracy Restored

The junta collapsed (1974) after the Cyprus crisis and Turkish invasion of the island. Democracy was restored under Constantine Karamanlis. A referendum (1974) abolished the monarchy, establishing a republic. The constitution of 1975 created a parliamentary democracy. Greece joined the European Economic Community (1981), the first Eastern Mediterranean member. Andreas Papandreou's PASOK won power (1981), bringing social reforms. The economy modernized but faced structural challenges.

1980s - Socialist Government

PASOK dominated the decade under Papandreou, implementing socialist policies, expanding the welfare state, and nationalizing industries. Relations with Turkey remained tense over Cyprus and the Aegean. Economic problems grew with inflation, deficits, and debt. Scandals emerged in the late 1980s. Greece remained the poorest EC member but benefited from European funds. Women's rights advanced significantly. The economy struggled with competitiveness. Political polarization increased.

1990s - Modernization Efforts

Governments alternated between conservatives and socialists, both pursuing EU convergence. Economic reforms aimed to meet Maastricht criteria for the euro. Greece qualified for the euro (2001, with later-questioned statistics). The economy grew strongly in the late 1990s. Athens was awarded the 2004 Olympics (1997). Relations with Turkey improved slightly. The Macedonia naming dispute created tensions. EU funds drove infrastructure development. Living standards rose, but structural economic problems persisted beneath surface prosperity.
