Italy: 1960-2000

1960s - Economic Miracle and Social Change

Italy experienced the "miracolo economico" (economic miracle) with rapid industrialization and growth. Mass migration from the rural south to the industrial north transformed society. The center-left coalition governments brought social reforms. The Second Vatican Council (1962-1965) modernized the Catholic Church. Student and worker protests erupted in 1968. Television became widespread, creating a more unified national culture. Tourism expanded significantly.

1970s - Years of Lead

The "anni di piombo" (years of lead) saw political violence from both far-left (Red Brigades) and far-right groups. Former Prime Minister Aldo Moro was kidnapped and murdered by the Red Brigades (1978). Divorce was legalized (1970) and upheld by referendum (1974). Abortion was legalized (1978). Economic crisis followed the oil shocks. Political instability continued with frequent government changes. The feminist movement gained strength.

1980s - Craxi Era and Mafia Wars

Socialist Bettino Craxi became prime minister (1983-1987), the longest-serving PM since World War II. Economic growth resumed, and Italy became the world's fifth-largest economy. The Mafia wars intensified, with assassinations of judges Giovanni Falcone and Paolo Borsellino (1992, though in early 1990s). Terrorism declined. Italy hosted the FIFA World Cup (1990). Consumer culture flourished, and Italian fashion and design gained global prominence.

1990s - Corruption Scandals and Reform

The "Mani Pulite" (Clean Hands) investigations (1992-1994) exposed massive political corruption, destroying the traditional party system. Media magnate Silvio Berlusconi entered politics, winning elections in 1994 and again in 2001. Italy joined the Eurozone despite high public debt. The Lira was replaced by the Euro (1999). Center-left governments under Romano Prodi and Massimo D'Alema pursued economic reforms. Anti-Mafia efforts intensified after the 1992 assassinations. Immigration increased significantly.
