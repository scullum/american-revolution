Netherlands: 1960-2000

1960s - Cultural Revolution

The Netherlands experienced rapid social liberalization. The "Provo" movement (1965-1967) challenged traditional authority through creative protests. Pillarization (verzuiling) began to break down as Dutch society secularized. Television became widespread, transforming culture. The wedding of Princess Beatrix to German diplomat Claus von Amsberg (1966) sparked protests due to his wartime past. Economic prosperity continued with strong welfare state expansion.

1970s - Progressive Policies

The Netherlands became known for progressive social policies. Abortion was decriminalized (1981, but policy shift began in 1970s). Drug policy evolved toward tolerance and harm reduction. Environmental consciousness grew after the North Sea flood disaster planning. The oil crisis (1973) impacted the economy. Suriname gained independence (1975). The Moluccan train hijackings (1975, 1977) highlighted integration challenges. Women's rights advanced significantly.

1980s - Economic Restructuring

The "Dutch Disease" economic crisis led to major reforms. The Wassenaar Agreement (1982) between unions and employers moderated wage growth and created the "polder model" of consensus politics. Unemployment peaked but then declined. Squatter movements protested housing shortages, culminating in riots during Queen Beatrix's coronation (1980). The peace movement protested NATO nuclear missiles. Euthanasia guidelines were established (1984). Economic recovery began mid-decade.

1990s - European Integration

The Netherlands was a founding member of the European Union (Maastricht Treaty, 1992, signed in the Dutch city). Amsterdam became a global financial center. The "purple coalition" governments (1994-2002) combined social democrats and liberals. Same-sex registered partnerships were introduced (1998). The Srebrenica massacre (1995) raised questions about Dutch peacekeeping forces. Immigration and integration became major political issues. The economy boomed, especially in technology and finance. The guilder was replaced by the euro (1999, circulation 2002).
