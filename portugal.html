Portugal: 1960-2000

1960s - Colonial Wars

António de Oliveira Salazar's authoritarian Estado Novo regime continued. Colonial wars began in Angola (1961), Guinea-Bissau (1963), and Mozambique (1964), draining resources and lives. Economic modernization accelerated despite dictatorship. Emigration to France and other European countries increased. Opposition was suppressed by the PIDE secret police. Salazar suffered a stroke (1968); Marcelo Caetano replaced him but continued authoritarian rule. Portugal remained isolated from democratic Europe.

1970s - Carnation Revolution

The Carnation Revolution (April 25, 1974) overthrew the dictatorship in a nearly bloodless military coup. Democracy was established amid political turmoil and attempted coups. The African colonies gained independence (1974-1975), and hundreds of thousands of retornados returned to Portugal. The economy struggled with instability and nationalization. The constitution (1976) established a democratic republic. Portugal joined the Council of Europe (1976). Political stability gradually emerged under democratic governments.

1980s - Democratic Consolidation

Democracy stabilized under various coalition governments. Portugal joined the European Economic Community (1986), transforming its economy and society. Mário Soares became the first civilian president in decades (1986). Economic modernization accelerated with EU funds. Infrastructure improved dramatically. The economy grew rapidly. Constitutional revisions (1982, 1989) removed socialist provisions and reduced the military's political role. Living standards rose significantly. Portugal began catching up with Western Europe.

1990s - European Integration

Portugal deepened its EU integration, qualifying for the euro (1999, circulation 2002). The economy continued modernizing, though regional disparities persisted. Expo '98 in Lisbon showcased Portugal's transformation. The Vasco da Gama Bridge, Europe's longest, opened (1998). Macau was returned to China (1999), ending Portugal's colonial era. Infrastructure development continued with EU funding. Tourism became a major industry. Democracy was fully consolidated. Portugal became a respected EU member, though economic challenges remained.
