Spain: 1960-2000

1960s - Late Franco Era Development

Spain remained under Francisco Franco's dictatorship but experienced rapid economic growth and modernization, the "Spanish miracle." Tourism boomed, bringing foreign currency and cultural influences. Technocrats in government pursued economic liberalization. Spain applied for EEC membership but was rejected due to its authoritarian government. Social changes accelerated despite political repression. Mass migration from rural areas to cities and to other European countries. The Catholic Church's influence began to wane.

1970s - Transition to Democracy

Franco died in 1975, ending 36 years of dictatorship. King Juan Carlos I oversaw the transition to democracy. Adolfo Suárez became prime minister (1976), legalizing political parties including the Communist Party. The first democratic elections since 1936 were held (1977). A new democratic constitution was approved by referendum (1978). The Basque separatist group ETA continued terrorist attacks. Regional autonomy was granted to Catalonia, the Basque Country, and other regions.

1980s - Democratic Consolidation

An attempted military coup failed (1981), strengthening democracy. The Socialist Party (PSOE) under Felipe González won elections (1982), governing until 1996. Spain joined NATO (1982) and the European Economic Community (1986). Economic modernization accelerated with EU membership. The 1992 Barcelona Olympics and Seville Expo showcased Spain's transformation. ETA terrorism continued. Regional nationalism grew stronger in Catalonia and the Basque Country.

1990s - European Integration

Spain became a founding member of the Eurozone, adopting the Euro (1999). The conservative Popular Party under José María Aznar won elections (1996). Economic growth was strong, with Spain catching up to European living standards. Infrastructure improved dramatically with EU funding. ETA declared a ceasefire (1998) but resumed attacks (1999). Immigration increased as Spain transformed from a country of emigration to immigration. Cultural renaissance continued with international recognition of Spanish cinema, literature, and cuisine.
