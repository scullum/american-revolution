Sweden: 1960-2000

1960s - Welfare State Peak

The Social Democratic "Swedish Model" reached its height, combining capitalism with extensive welfare programs. The economy boomed with full employment. Universal healthcare and pension systems expanded. The shift from right-hand to left-hand traffic occurred (Dagen H, 1967). Immigration increased to meet labor demands. Neutrality continued during the Cold War. Cultural liberalization accelerated, with Sweden becoming known for progressive social policies. Television became universal.

1970s - Reform and Debate

Constitutional reform (1974) reduced the monarch's role to purely ceremonial. King Carl XVI Gustaf ascended the throne (1973). The Social Democrats lost power briefly (1976-1982) for the first time in 44 years. The nuclear power debate intensified after the Three Mile Island accident. Women's rights advanced significantly. Olof Palme led debates on international solidarity and Third World development. The economy faced challenges from oil shocks. Immigration policy became more restrictive.

1980s - Assassination and Change

Prime Minister Olof Palme was assassinated (1986) in Stockholm, shocking the nation; the murder remains unsolved. A referendum (1980) narrowly approved nuclear power with phase-out provisions. Financial deregulation began, moving away from strict controls. The economy overheated in the late 1980s. Environmental consciousness grew. The welfare state faced increasing costs. Immigration from outside Europe increased. The Social Democrats remained dominant but faced growing challenges.

1990s - Crisis and EU Membership

A severe banking and economic crisis (1990-1993) forced major reforms and austerity measures. Unemployment soared from 2% to 10%. Sweden joined the European Union (1995) after a referendum, ending traditional neutrality policy. The welfare state was trimmed but preserved. The economy recovered strongly in the late 1990s, driven by technology companies like Ericsson. The Öresund Bridge to Denmark was completed (2000). Immigration and integration became major political issues. The far-right made electoral gains.
