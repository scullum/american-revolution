United Kingdom: 1960-2000

1960s - Social Revolution and Decline

The "Swinging Sixties" brought cultural revolution, with London as a global center of fashion and music. Harold Wilson's Labour government (1964-1970) pursued social reforms and technological modernization. The death penalty was abolished (1965). Decolonization continued with independence for many African and Caribbean territories. Economic challenges grew as traditional industries declined. The UK applied to join the European Economic Community but was vetoed by France (1963, 1967).

1970s - Economic Crisis

The UK joined the European Economic Community (1973) under Conservative Prime Minister Edward Heath. Economic crisis deepened with inflation, strikes, and the three-day work week (1974). Labour returned under Wilson, then James Callaghan. The "Winter of Discontent" (1978-79) saw widespread strikes. North Sea oil production began. Troubles in Northern Ireland intensified with Bloody Sunday (1972) and ongoing violence. Devolution referendums in Scotland and Wales failed (1979).

1980s - Thatcher Revolution

Margaret Thatcher became the first female prime minister (1979), serving until 1990. Her government pursued privatization, deregulation, and confrontation with trade unions, notably during the miners' strike (1984-85). The Falklands War (1982) boosted her popularity. Financial deregulation transformed London into a global financial center ("Big Bang," 1986). Unemployment rose sharply in industrial regions. The IRA conducted bombing campaigns. Thatcher opposed closer European integration.

1990s - New Labour

John Major succeeded Thatcher (1990-1997). The UK declined to join the Eurozone. The Channel Tunnel opened (1994). The IRA ceasefire (1994) and Good Friday Agreement (1998) brought hope for peace in Northern Ireland. Tony Blair's "New Labour" won a landslide victory (1997), pursuing a "Third Way" between socialism and capitalism. Devolution created Scottish Parliament and Welsh Assembly (1999). The UK participated in NATO operations in the Balkans. The economy grew steadily through the decade.
